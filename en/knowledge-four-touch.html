Press, knead, glide and fine touch: what is the difference?

Distinguish four techniques by contact area, direction and rhythm, and learn to communicate preferences beforehand.

Understand the four techniques through visible movements

Pressing, kneading, gliding and small plucking motions are the four manual touch styles shown in Jiang Meridian Wellness's current brand videos. Beginners may find their names alone difficult to visualize. A more intuitive approach is to observe whether contact stays relatively steady, whether movement follows a continuous direction, and whether its range and speed vary. These descriptions help identify illustrated movements, not prescribe how every technique must be performed.

Pressing: a steady sensation of contact

The pressing scene shows concentrated, relatively steady contact. Notice the transition between contact and release rather than simply how much force the hands seem to use. Some people like quiet, regular contact, while others prefer a wider area. If you experience a sensation you dislike during a session, say so immediately rather than waiting for the movement to finish.

Kneading: rhythm and range of motion

The kneading illustration shows more noticeable small circular movements. Compared with pressing, contact changes more continuously on screen, but neither style is necessarily lighter or stronger. A person's preferences can also vary with circumstances that day. You can say 'a little slower, please' or 'I'd prefer lighter contact' without knowing technical terms.

Gliding and plucking: direction and detail

The gliding scene makes the direction of continuous movement easy to see, while the plucking scene emphasizes changes in a smaller contact area. Differences between these styles should not be interpreted as a claim that one can address a particular physical problem. The videos are brand illustrations, not instructions on acupoint positions, pressure angles or procedures. Confirm actual services with the provider before receiving them.

How to choose the experience you prefer

Watch the four videos, note the rhythms you like and consider which area you wish to relax today. If you dislike a particular type of contact, prefer to remain clothed or prefer touch through a towel, make that clear before starting. When unsure about pressure, there is no need to start with intense contact. The recipient has the right to request changes or stop. Choices should be based on informed consent and comfort, not another person's definition of a noticeable sensation.

How to interpret movement differences in the videos

Camera distance and angle may make movements appear more pronounced. Rather than guessing how much force the provider uses, treat the video as an introduction to the style. To learn more about a technique, visit its individual page, then inquire about actual service content and personal boundaries.

FAQ: Do I need to receive all four techniques?

Not necessarily. The four styles introduce the brand's manual techniques; they are not a fixed package or mandatory at every session. Confirm desired content and available time before deciding. Traditional bodywork aims at everyday relaxation; for physical abnormalities or unexplained discomfort, seek appropriate medical evaluation first. Each technique has its own illustrative video page on this site, making it easy to learn about its visual features before inquiring.
