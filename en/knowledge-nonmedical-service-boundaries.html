Understanding the boundaries of non-medical traditional bodywork

Identify its role, service descriptions, advertising boundaries and when medical care is needed.

First examine the purpose and scope of the service

Jiang Meridian Wellness presents itself as everyday relaxation and non-medical traditional wellness. Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare rules and advertising guidance state that traditional wellness providers cannot perform medical acts or solicit clients by implying medical services. Readers can first assess whether the stated purpose is consistent with a non-medical service.

Technique descriptions are not medical prescriptions

The website may describe contact characteristics and rhythms of established pressing, kneading, pushing and plucking techniques. These texts, images and videos are not disease assessments or medical instructions for readers to perform themselves. Traditional technique names do not establish a provider's medical qualifications or ability to address a specific health problem. Qualified medical professionals should provide medical care when needed.

Watch for promises in advertisements

Distinguish general descriptions of feelings from promises to treat disease. Even gentler wording should not imply medical effects. Photos and videos should not be interpreted as evidence of outcomes; AI imagery on Jiang's site is illustrative, not actual client testimony, service records or evidence of a venue.

Communicate clearly before the service

When booking, confirm service details, fees, location, timing, contact areas, draping and how to stop. If a provider is unwilling to explain important information, defer arrangements. During the service, you may still request adjustments or stop; you need not accept contact to which you have not consented.

When to seek professional assessment first

Seek medical assessment first for new, persistent, severe or unexplained discomfort; seek immediate help for acute serious situations as appropriate. Do not delay necessary care while waiting for traditional wellness or independently stop treatment arranged by medical professionals. Service inquiries cannot replace these decisions.

How to verify official information

For questions about traditional wellness business and advertising boundaries, consult the Ministry of Health and Welfare's current rules or ask the local health authority. Regulations may change; do not rely only on old teaching materials, social posts or hearsay. This article is general service information, not individual legal or medical advice.

Ask first if information is incomplete

Some descriptions display technique names or imagery without explaining actual services. Ask whether the service is for everyday relaxation, how contact is arranged and whether it can be stopped at any time. If advertising and actual explanations differ, defer the service and consult the local authority if necessary.

FAQ: Do professional articles establish medical qualifications?

No. Knowledge articles and medical qualifications are separate matters. Article count, design style, traditional terms and AI imagery do not make a brand website a medical institution. Actual services and qualifications require independent, verifiable evidence and cannot be inferred from third-party material.
