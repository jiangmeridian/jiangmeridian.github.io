TRADITION & TOUCH

Understanding meridians and manual relaxation

Meridians are a traditional East Asian concept of the body; modern traditional bodywork is distinct from medical practice.

Jiang Meridian Wellness | Website editorial draft · November 26, 2026

What are meridians?

In traditional East Asian thought, meridians are part of a theoretical vocabulary for understanding the body. Introducing this cultural concept does not mean that it corresponds to blood vessels, nerves or other visible structures in modern anatomy, nor that manual techniques can treat disease.

Understanding differences through the sensation of touch

Pressing, kneading, gliding and small plucking motions can be understood through contact position, direction and rhythm. The pressing illustration shows relatively steady contact, kneading shows circular motion, gliding shows direction, and plucking shows smaller movements. This is a way to recognize styles of touch, not a medical tutorial to follow for applying pressure.

Four aspects of manual touch to observe

Begin with what you can feel and describe yourself. The following aspects are general communication tools, not acupoint instructions; you need not know meridian names beforehand.

Contact area

Contact with the palm and contact over a smaller area can feel different to each person. You can say whether you prefer broader or more concentrated contact.

Direction of movement

Stationary, circular and continuous moving contact do not feel identical. Watch the four illustrative videos and describe which style you prefer.

Pace and rhythm

Some people prefer a steady, slow rhythm; others enjoy variation. Current feelings and communication between both parties should guide the session.

Pressure and boundaries

Pain is not a measure of manual technique quality. If uncomfortable, you can ask at any time for lighter pressure, a different contact area or a stop.

Clarify expectations before the session

You can describe the area you wish to relax today, whether you prefer quiet or conversation, your preferred pressure and how you wish to be covered. The mutually agreed boundaries matter more than seeking intense sensations.

Understanding boundaries helps set clear expectations

Traditional bodywork focuses on relaxation and easing everyday fatigue. For unexplained or persistent physical discomfort, seek appropriate medical evaluation. The manual technique illustrations do not represent diagnosis or therapeutic effects.
